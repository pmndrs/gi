@pmndrs/gi · Examples

Real-time global illumination for WebGPU: light bounces off walls and floors, and follows moving lights, objects and the sun. Each example is the shortest code that shows one feature.

These examples need WebGPU in Chrome or Edge 113+, or Safari 26+. Drag to orbit in the DDGI and radiance-cache examples, or ride the camera rail in Split Radiance Cascades.

Which method for what

DDGI · Start here. A box of probes you place, like three's LightProbeGrid. The shortest set-up, the widest range of GPUs, its work inside a budget you set, saved bakes and a static fallback.
Radiance cache · Probes on what the camera sees, over a world cache: no volume to place. For when a fixed grid is too coarse for the view, or no box fits the scene, and the device has the memory. Heavier and less tuned than DDGI today.
Split Radiance Cascades · A separate study: a native WebGPU solver compared with a three.js reference, on a gallery you ride through.

The guide DDGI or the radiance cache? compares the two, with numbers and their sources. Each example has an explainer panel: what you're seeing, how it works and what to try.

Radiance cache

Through @pmndrs/gi/radiance-cache, wired into three's post-processing pipeline with @pmndrs/gi/three: screen probes over a world cache, with no volume to place.

C1
No volume needed
The cache alone in a sunlit courtyard, with no probe box: screen probes where the camera looks, their rays ending in world-cache cells only where they land.
C2
Contact detail
The cache and DDGI wiped on one view of a shelf of boxes: screen probes keep the light where objects meet, a probe grid blurs it. Two probe spacings and a difference view.
C3
Light through a doorway
A closed room lit only through a doorway needs several bounces. Switch the cache's second bounce between GI-1.2's own rays, DDGI underneath and SHaRC, and watch the far room's brightness.
