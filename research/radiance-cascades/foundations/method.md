# From radiance intervals to Split RC

Alexander Sannikov’s original manuscript describes the penumbra criterion. Nearby lighting and occlusion vary quickly across space, while distant geometry can subtend a small angle. A cascade therefore spends more samples on positions for short intervals and more on directions for long intervals. The storage and ray counts depend on the number of spatial and angular dimensions and the chosen branching factors. A 2D demo’s cost bound cannot be carried into a 3D GPU budget without recalculating those counts. [Original manuscript, pinned TeX](https://github.com/Raikiri/RadianceCascadesPaper/blob/9d1fd290bb08f2589024ce83b75a7cc3323c6a4d/RadianceCascades.tex)

A directional interval stores arriving radiance and how much light passes through it. For a near pair `(Jₙ, Tₙ)` and the next farther pair `(J𝒻, T𝒻)`, the ordered composition is `(Jₙ + Tₙ × J𝒻, Tₙ × T𝒻)`. An empty interval has zero radiance and unit transmittance. An opaque black blocker has zero radiance and zero transmittance. These give two small regression cases for merge order, empty initialization and light leaks. [Original manuscript](https://github.com/Raikiri/RadianceCascadesPaper/blob/9d1fd290bb08f2589024ce83b75a7cc3323c6a4d/RadianceCascades.tex) · [Chandra split and merge code](https://github.com/entropylost/chandra/blob/9af00e8de135a4126cb137d1ec62b451bd6b8e37/src/algorithm/rc.rs)

Rouli Freeman, Alexander Sannikov and Joe Pitt-Francis describe Split RC in the October revision of their paper. It places sparse probes in world space. Rays originate on visible surfaces, and a hit distance identifies which cascade interval receives radiance. A ray also supplies evidence that it passed through the nearer intervals. This reuses a trace across levels. Coarser intervals are merged into finer intervals, then directional samples are integrated for diffuse surface lighting. The paper evaluates both single-frame and accumulated estimates. [Split Radiance Cascades, rev. 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20384v2) · [Chandra final shading](https://github.com/entropylost/chandra/blob/9af00e8de135a4126cb137d1ec62b451bd6b8e37/src/algorithm/splat.rs)

Probe placement, ray traversal and accumulation are separate choices. A screen-space placement can launch an offscreen-capable world-space query. A world-space cache can still miss an emitter if its traversal backend only sees camera depth. Camera cuts, occluder motion and light changes therefore exercise different failure modes. Sannikov’s sparse 3D prototype illustrates this by combining visible-surface allocation in a GPU hash table with screen-space ray marching.
