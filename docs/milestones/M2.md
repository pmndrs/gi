**How to read the numbers:**
- **"a / b" frame pairs** are two separate measurements of the same check: two runs, two steps, or the view and the whole field. Each row says which.
- **p50 / p95:** the median, and the value 95% of frames stay under.
- **VALID:** a timing run that met the clock rule (it started below 70 C, and under 5% of its samples were slowed by heat).
- **Budget v2:** DDGI updates a fixed number of probes each frame, measured once at load so they cost about 2 ms.
- **`gi/direct`:** the pass that traces shadow rays toward lights from each probe ray's hit.
- **`ddgi/mark`:** the pass that works out how much each probe's light changed.

## What works

- **Light changes reach the GI quickly (T2.4a–d).**
  - The platform tells DDGI the old and the new light. `ddgi/mark` turns that into how much each probe's lighting changed, worked out from the light's own numbers, so ray noise can't fake a change.
  - A changed probe forgets part of its history, in proportion to the change.
  - A GPU scheduler puts the most changed probes near the camera first.
  - For changes that touch the whole scene (the sun, the sky), 1 probe in 8 is a "key probe": keys are traced first and pass their change on to the probes between them.
  - Still scenes mark 0 probes and match M1.
- **Moving objects, pulled forward from M3 (T2.M1).**
  - `setTransforms` takes 10,000 transforms in 1.4 ms, inside real three frames too.
  - A moved cabinet is within 5% of settled in under 1 s.
  - A closing door keeps the dark room's leak under 1%.
  - Creating and destroying 1,000 instances recompiles nothing.
- **Shadow rays in their own pass (T2.0, T2.2).** `gi/direct` roughly doubled the probes Sponza fits in the 2 ms budget (2.03×).
- **Many lights (T2.1, T2.3).**
  - The light buffer grows without recompiling.
  - A world-space light grid picks which lights get shadow rays. `sponza-candles` (1,024 candles) now sets up in 1.6× plain Sponza's time; before T2.3 it took 958 s.
  - Each probe update still costs 2.2–2.5× as much as on plain Sponza, against a 1.3× bar: a known miss. Unranged candles make nearly every surface need a shadow ray.
- **Light groups (T2.5).** Each group gets its own irradiance layer, so a dimmer or colour change across a group shows in the same frame with no update. A volume holds up to 6 layers.
- **Time of day (T2.6a, T2.6b, T2.4d).**
  - The sky is re-baked on the GPU.
  - The sun's colour and brightness change in the same frame through the sun layer. It is off by default (lead ruling, waiting on Dennis): it costs 1.055× per probe update, and its memory, +35.5 MB on Sponza, came in over the 20 MB estimate.
  - A change in the sun's direction is re-traced, key probes first.
  - Two of the design's ideas lost to their own decision checks: the "instant sky" was dropped, and "far probes owe 2" stayed a fallback behind key probes.
- **Faster start (T2.12–T2.14).**
  - First GI comes from GPU-built mesh trees; tinybvh's trees swap in per mesh when its worker finishes. On Bistro the GPU trees alone trace at 0.981× tinybvh's time, so whether the swap is still needed is a question for M3.
  - The scene copy is packed on the GPU.
  - Kernels compile only when first used. Cornell starts in 1.44 s with a fresh browser profile.
  - Sponza's main-thread scene copy (816 ms against a 150 ms bar) is a known miss.
- **The update stays in budget after a big change (T2.18).** While a change is still being taken in, DDGI uses a second, smaller probe count. The 60 frames after Sponza's sun turn cost what still frames cost (0.99×). The price: the view now settles at frame 15, exactly on its bar (it was 13).
- **The viewer (T2.8).**
  - Light gizmos, sliders, groups, time of day and scripted motions from the scene files.
  - A response meter that plots the GI-only brightness of a picked area and writes it to CSV.
  - A **native compare page** (`compare.html`) that plays our recording and native RTXGI's recording of the same motion in sync, with both curves on one plot.
- **The native harness (T2.7)** plays the scene files' motions and a time-of-day sun, reads the shared sky file, and records frames and curves.
- **Clean viewing references (T2.16).** The six grainy Cycles views were re-rendered, and GI noise is now at most 3.2% per pixel.

## What it cost to get here

- **The plan held.** Change marks, the GPU scheduler and key probes gave fast responses everywhere except a dim lamp in sunlit Sponza and a room going ten times darker.
- **Fixed bars inside noise.** T2.18's 2.0 / 2.2 ms bars sat inside the calibration's own spread, so passing or failing came down to the run. The check now compares with the same run's still frames (lead ruling, DECISIONS 2026-11-08).
- **Two outside problems looked like our own.**
  - Bistro's GI frame took 545 ms because its uncompressed textures (5.1 GB in three) plus DDGI's 0.6 GB overflowed the 6 GB laptop GPU. With textures capped at 1024 it takes 47 ms (T2.17).
  - The clock fell when Chrome went from 156 to 157.
- **The native comparison needed its own fixes.**
  - The harness turned three's "no cut-off" range of 0 into a radius of 0, so native's Sponza lamp lit nothing until T2.10 found it.
  - The texture sky didn't reach native's probes until T2.7's review fixed it.

## What isn't done, or isn't known

- **Known misses, for Dennis to agree** (DECISIONS, with the lead's recommendations):
  - Sponza's lamp step at today's low clock (2026-11-09);
  - two-room's lamp leaving the lit room (2026-11-09);
  - the light grid's build at 2,048 cells (2026-11-09);
  - the Intel key-probe turn (2026-11-09);
  - `sponza-candles`' cost per probe update, 2.2–2.5× against 1.3× (T2.3, 2026-11-07);
  - Sponza's main-thread scene copy, 816 ms against 150, and Bistro's, 1,862 ms against 200 (T2.13, 2026-11-08);
  - Sponza's sun turn with the sun layer on, frame 16 against 15 (T2.8, 2026-11-08);
  - the slowest frames right after a change, p95 at 1.05× still frames (T2.18, 2026-11-08).
- **The clock.** Our pages ran at about 800 MHz, the Bistro re-runs at a fixed 1,200 MHz. Re-timing the clock-sensitive gates waits on Dennis's answer (DECISIONS 2026-11-09).
- **Bistro** needs compressed textures to fit 6 GB cards. Dennis chose GPUTex (DECISIONS 2026-11-09), and it is an M3 task; until then the test pages cap Bistro's textures at 1024. Bistro's calibration also lands anywhere from 52 to 116 probes, and its budget check fails on the page's own rule. No page measures a Bistro response.
- **Native's Sponza GI is about 8× Cycles'.** Native's direct light is already 2.9× Cycles', close to π, so the sun's units in our harness settings are the first suspect. Ours matches Cycles' indirect pass and our own path tracer. On two-room ours is 1.6× brighter than native; native clamping its lamp falloff at 1 m may be part of that (not measured).
- **Area lights** are still to come (Dennis: "lets not abandon them"; m2-lights.md, "Later").
- **Not measured:**
  - a whole frame on the library's default composite with the update in the same frame;
  - a visible (headed) Chrome window;
  - native on D3D12;
  - the Apple M5 Pro and iOS devices.
- **Flakes:**
  - the Intel still hangs now and then in batches of 4 pages; batches of 3 or fewer are safer;
  - three unit tests time out at 5 s when the CPU is busy, and pass alone.
