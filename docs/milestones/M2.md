## What works

- **Light changes reach the GI quickly (T2.4a–d).** The platform tells DDGI the old and the new light. A GPU kernel (`ddgi/mark`) turns that into how much each probe's lighting changed, worked out from the light's own numbers, so ray noise can't fake a change. A changed probe's history is cut in proportion, and a GPU scheduler puts the most changed probes near the camera first. For changes that touch the whole scene (the sun, the sky), 1 probe in 8 is a key probe: keys are traced first and pass their change on to the probes between them. Still scenes mark 0 probes and match M1.
- **Moving objects, pulled forward from M3 (T2.M1).** `setTransforms` takes 10,000 transforms in 1.4 ms, inside real three frames too. A moved cabinet is within 5% of settled in under 1 s. A closing door keeps the dark room's leak under 1%. Creating and destroying 1,000 instances recompiles nothing.
- **Shadow rays in their own pass (T2.0, T2.2).** `gi/direct` in platform hit shading roughly doubled the probes Sponza fits in the 2 ms budget (2.03×).
- **Many lights (T2.1, T2.3).** The light buffer grows without recompiling. A world-space light grid picks lights for shadow rays, so `sponza-candles` (1,024 candles) sets up in 1.6× plain Sponza's time. It was 958 s before T2.3.
- **Light groups (T2.5).** Each group gets its own irradiance layer, so a dimmer or colour change across a group shows in the same frame with no update. A volume holds up to 6 layers.
- **Time of day (T2.6a, T2.6b, T2.4d).**
  - The sky is re-baked on the GPU.
  - The sun's colour and brightness change in the same frame through the sun layer. It is off by default (lead ruling, waiting on Dennis), because it costs 1.055× per probe update.
  - A change in the sun's direction is re-traced, key probes first.
  - The "instant sky" was dropped by its own decision check.
- **Faster start (T2.12–T2.14).**
  - First GI comes from GPU-built mesh trees; tinybvh's trees swap in per mesh when the worker finishes.
  - The scene copy is packed on the GPU.
  - Kernels compile only when first used.
  - Cornell starts in 1.44 s from cold.
  - Sponza's main-thread scene copy (816 ms against a 150 ms bar) is a known miss.
- **The update stays in budget after a big change (T2.18).** While a change is still being taken in, DDGI uses a second, smaller probe count, so the 60 frames after Sponza's sun turn cost what still frames cost: 0.99×.
- **The viewer (T2.8).**
  - Light gizmos, sliders, groups, time of day and scripted motions from the scene files.
  - A response meter that plots the GI-only brightness of a picked area and writes it to CSV.
  - A **native compare page** (`compare.html`) that plays our recording and native RTXGI's recording of the same motion in sync, with both curves on one plot.
- **The native harness (T2.7)** plays the scene files' motions and a time-of-day sun, reads the shared sky file, and records frames and curves.
- **Clean viewing references (T2.16).** The six grainy Cycles views were re-rendered: GI noise is now at most 3.2% per pixel.

## What it cost to get here

- **The plan held.** Change marks, the GPU scheduler and key probes gave fast responses everywhere except a dim lamp in sunlit Sponza. Two of the design's ideas lost to their own decision checks and were dropped: the instant sky (T2.6b) and "far probes owe 2" as the main path.
- **Fixed bars inside noise.** T2.18's 2.0 / 2.2 ms bars sat inside the calibration's own spread, so a pass or a fail came down to the run. The bar now compares against the same run's still frames (lead ruling, DECISIONS 2026-11-08).
- **Two outside problems hid as our own.** Bistro's GI frame took 545 ms because its uncompressed textures (5.1 GB in three) plus DDGI's 0.6 GB overflowed the 6 GB laptop GPU. With textures capped at 1024 it takes 47 ms (T2.17). The clock fell when Chrome went from 156 to 157.
- **The native comparison needed its own fixes.** The harness turned three's "no cut-off" range of 0 into a radius of 0, so native's Sponza lamp lit nothing until T2.10 found it. The texture sky didn't reach native's probes until T2.7's review fixed it.

## What isn't done, or isn't known

- **Known misses, for Dennis to agree:**
  - Sponza's main-thread scene copy (816 ms against 150; Bistro 1,862 ms against 200; T2.13);
  - Sponza's lamp step at today's low clock;
  - the light grid's build at 2,048 cells;
  - Sponza's sun turn with the sun layer on (frame 16 against 15; T2.8);
  - the slowest frames right after a change (p95 1.05× still).
- **The clock.** Everything was measured at about 800 MHz. Re-timing the clock-sensitive gates waits on Dennis's answer about Chrome 157.
- **Bistro** needs compressed textures to fit 6 GB cards. Dennis chose GPUTex (DECISIONS 2026-11-09), and it's an M3 task; until then the test pages cap Bistro's textures at 1024. Bistro's calibration also jumps around (a fixed trace cost of 1–1.5 ms), and no page measures a Bistro response.
- **Native's Sponza GI is about 8× Cycles'.** Its direct light is already 2.9× Cycles', close to π, so the sun's units in our harness settings are the first suspect. Ours matches Cycles' indirect pass and our own path tracer. Two-room differs by 1.6× (native's falloff is clamped at 1 m; not measured).
- **Not measured:**
  - a whole frame on the library's default composite path with the update running in the same frame;
  - a visible (headed) Chrome window;
  - native on D3D12;
  - the Apple M5 Pro and iOS devices.
- **Flakes:**
  - the Intel still hangs now and then in batches of 4 pages; batches of 3 or fewer are safer;
  - three unit tests time out at 5 s when the CPU is busy, and pass alone.
