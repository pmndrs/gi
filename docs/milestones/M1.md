## What works

- **One shared sky (T1.1):** one HDR sky file per scene, used by the viewer, Cycles, the native harness and the platform.
- **The platform (T1.2–T1.4):** a scene copy on the GPU, our own ray tracer as a platform module (tinybvh trees built in a worker; the GPU top level refits every frame), and hit shading (sun, sky, lights, shadow rays).
- **DDGI (T1.5a, T1.5b):** probes in a grid with octahedral irradiance and distance atlases, inline tracing, probe classification and relocation (subgroups first, with a tested fallback), unlimited bounces from the field.
- **Budget v2 (T1.9b):** DDGI updates a fixed number of probes a frame, measured once at load with the clocks up, so a quiet GPU's power saving no longer starves it. It steps down when frames are late (also in the laptop's hot state) and recovers; a floor keeps a full sweep within 4 s; a display rate that drops for good (a battery cap) is followed.
- **Stability (T1.12):** each probe averages over a history that knows the time since its last update. Cornell's flicker dropped from 0.56% to 0.095% mean change a frame; Sponza settles in seconds after a change instead of minutes. A warm-up ramp brings Cornell to −7% right after loading (it was −26%). The viewer has debug views for checking it by eye: the probe atlases, probes coloured by how settled they are, direct against indirect, and change per frame.
- **Plain WebGPU (T1.6):** `examples/webgpu/hello` renders Cornell with GI.
- **three.js (T1.7, T1.14):**
  - the simple path is `GIVolume`, shaped like three's `LightProbeGrid`: `new GIVolume(...)`, `position.set`, `scene.add`, `await volume.start(renderer, scene)`. That's 5 lines, as many as three's own LightProbeGrid example; GI updates at render time, with no per-frame call;
  - the fast path is a drop-in node on three's own pipeline, `ddgi(scenePass, volume)` with the `giAlbedo` MRT node, built like three's `ssgi()` and `ao()`. It shades GI once per pixel and matches the per-fragment image within 0.08% mean;
  - the intended pipeline runs it through `@pmndrs/upscaler`, with three's GTAO on the GI term (Dennis, DECISIONS 2026-11-06);
  - `examples/three/` shows both, and [docs/api/three.md](../api/three.md) documents them.
- **The viewer (T1.8):** DDGI next to three's probe grid and VXGI, with stats, probe spheres by state, Compare against Cycles, and hover help on every control.
- **HD references (T1.13):** every Cycles reference is 1920×1080, stored with its full camera, and the viewer's Compare loads that camera.
- **Sponza add-ons (T1.10):** the curtains are part of `sponza`; ivy and trees make `sponza-full`. The candles scene (`sponza-candles`, 1,024 lights) moved to M2.
- **BVH lane:** tinybvh in a worker (B0, B0b), the GPU top level (B1a), GPU LBVH (B1) and GPU H-PLOC (B2; Sponza 46.6 ms against 50) are merged. Compressed wide nodes (B3) were measured and dropped by Dennis.

## What it cost to get here

- **The laptop's clocks.** A light load lets the RTX drop to a low-power state, and a sustained load heats it to 87 C, after which it holds about 500 MHz. Both inflated every millisecond for a while. Budget v2, the cool-start lock, and the clock rule came out of that. Dennis then asked us to stop measuring so much ("we obsessed a lot about the timing"), and timing is now one VALID run per gate.
- **Sponza's curtains.** The +23% gap was fold-scale light that 1 m probes can't follow, not bad data. GTAO on the GI term fixes it in the composite path; nested finer volumes around cloth are the M3 quality route.
- **Per-fragment GI in three** cost +12 ms on Sponza (sub-pixel triangles shade GI many times per pixel), which led to the composite.

## What isn't done, or isn't known

- **Some viewing-only reference bookmarks are noisy on GI** (per pixel: point-light Cornell cameras 2 and 3 about 9–10%, the dark room 6.2%, two-room through the doorway 3.5%, textured Sponza at 512 samples about 14%; every reference is under 2% over 8×8 blocks). The viewer shows each one's sample count and noise. The accuracy references are clean. Re-rendering the noisy views (or denoised GI copies for viewing) is a follow-up.
- **Sponza's FLIP** (0.283 against 0.15) misses: its mean is within ±10%, but the per-block error is large around the curtains and fine geometry (8×8 block error p95 114%). Nested finer volumes around cloth (M3) are the quality route.
- **Budget v2's calibration once handed Sponza 26 probes a frame instead of about 290** during the flicker check (not reproduced in the next run); worth a look in M2.
- **Sponza startup** (4.5 s) misses 2 s: file loading and the CPU mesh trees. Fast first GI from the GPU builders is in M2, lower priority.
- **A measured whole-frame p50/p95** with GI updating and shading in one frame.
- **Native three without the upscaler** doesn't fit 1.0 ms with occlusion; GTAO is opt-in there.
- **The viewer** still has its own copy of the warm-up; switching it to `volume.start` is a follow-up.
- **Not tested:** the Apple M5 Pro and iOS devices; an interactive (headed) session of the debug views; Intel timings (not gated).
- Known flakes: the Intel hung (`DXGI_ERROR_DEVICE_HUNG`) twice in two days during long back-to-back GPU suites, a pattern to look at; `tools/assets/fetch.test.ts` under load.
- Skipped (lean T1.9): the Bistro exterior baseline re-measure.
