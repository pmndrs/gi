- **Accepted by Dennis 2026-11-05 (entry above): budget v2. Work (probes per frame) is the primary control, calibrated once at load with the clocks up; milliseconds become a ceiling used only when the frame rate drops; a floor bounds the sweep time** (M1 lead, after the T1.9 quiet window). The full proposal, with how a host picks the number per tier, what happens when the GPU is saturated, the redefined measurements and what T1.9 re-measures, is in `docs/design/ddgi.md`, "Budget v2". It supersedes the T1.9a entry "The millisecond budget uses a fixed + per-probe cost model" and the "budget holds" definition if accepted. The earlier, smaller version of this proposal follows:
  - *The problem:* a GPU-millisecond budget can't tell "this work is expensive" from "the GPU is in its low-power state". On a quiet machine at 60 Hz the RTX sits in P8, so 2 ms bought 2–3 Sponza probes a frame (a full sweep every 162 s) against 30–43 when other apps kept the clocks up.
  - *But the budget's purpose is to protect the frame time.* A GPU idling in P8 has room to spare, so spending more of it there costs the frame nothing.
  - **Proposal 1, a floor:** DDGI never updates fewer than `minProbesPerFrame` probes, default "auto" = enough for a full sweep within a set time (e.g. 4 s at 60 Hz, which is 81 probes on Sponza and 8 on Cornell), whatever the ms budget says. The ms budget can raise the batch above the floor; `budget.units` stays a hard cap above everything. This bounds convergence time on every machine.
  - **Proposal 2, measuring:** SPEC's "update ≤ 2 ms" and "GI adds ≤ 3 ms p50" are judged with the GPU clocks up (back to back, or a GPU-bound frame), because that's when the milliseconds matter. The 60 Hz light-load numbers are reported as information, with the clock note.

- **What "indirect light" means against Cycles in a scene with a sky** (M1 lead, from T1.9's Sponza check). DDGI's field holds every light that reaches a probe by a ray: bounces, and also light seen straight from the sky and from emitters. Cycles' indirect pass leaves out the sky and emitters seen straight from the surface; those count as direct. So the reference for DDGI is **indirect + direct − a lights-only direct render** (everything except the analytic lights' direct light, which the renderer draws itself). `render.py` makes it as `cycles-*-gi.exr`. Same logic as the point-light Cornell (2026-11-04). Cornell and two-room have no sky, so their numbers don't change. On Sponza, the raw indirect pass would show +101%; against the composite, +26%.
  - **When it was set:** after the raw +101% had been seen (the composite and the +26% landed in 5434142, this entry 11 minutes later in e7a3a95), and before any fix. Its reason follows from the 2026-11-04 point-light Cornell entry, not from the result.

- **The millisecond budget uses a fixed + per-probe cost model built from medians** (T1.9a). Small batches are limited by latency, not by probe count, so a model proportional to probes shrank Sponza's batch in a spiral. Outside GPU spikes biased a mean-based estimate, so the typical frame landed at about 60% of the budget. Details: the slope is a least-squares line through per-batch-size medians; the fixed part is the median of the last 31 frames' (ms − slope × probes). After the first frame the batch is steered by the median time of recent frames of a similar size (×0.8–×1.25 per frame; be46b60, being revised after its re-review). *Revisit if* a quiet machine shows the batch oscillating.
- **"The budget holds" means the median per-frame update time at 60 Hz is within ±10% of the budget.** Single frames include other programs' GPU work (next entry). T1.9a measured within ±10% on Cornell, two-room, Sponza and Bistro exterior (RTX; ±5% in its own runs, +7% on the review's Sponza run).
- **GPU timestamps on this laptop likely include other programs' GPU work** (circumstantial: GPU performance counters and the timing pattern; not yet checked with Dennis's apps closed). Something lands every 50 ms (RTX +2.5 ms, Intel +1.3 ms): Dennis's Chrome GPU process uses 17–27% of the RTX and the Windows compositor 7–12%. - **The three.js adapter, as built in T1.7** (M1 lead):
  - **Engine neutrality is judged with Lambert materials:** means within 0.5%, and at most 0.5% of pixels off by more than 2%. three's standard material scales indirect diffuse by (1 − F_ss − F_ms), so it can't match a plain diffuse host. Measured: means within 1e-8, 0 pixels differ, on both GPUs.
  - **Every material is double-sided for GI by default,** whatever three's `side`, with a per-material opt-out (`material.userData.giSingleSided`). Most glTF materials are FrontSide, and single-sided GI would let rays pass through the backs of walls, which breaks DDGI's inside-geometry detection (contract `doubleSided`).
  - **One extra queue submit per frame:** r187 makes and submits its own encoders with no hook, so `ThreeGI.update` submits the GI work just before `renderer.render`. Queue order puts it first. This departs from the contract's "no extra submits", for the three adapter only.
  - **Timing of changes:** moves reach GI one frame late by default (the adapter doesn't update world matrices itself; doing so would repeat three's 1 ms per frame on Bistro interior).
  - **Change tracking does a fixed amount of work per frame** (52a7300, halved in a97e29a): the scene is walked 64 objects a frame; objects seen changing are checked every frame for 60 frames; 8 geometry and material edit checks per frame; lights every frame. A static object's first move is seen within ceil(objects / 64) frames (Bistro interior 41, about 0.7 s), or in the next frame when the host calls `markChanged(object)`. Both budgets are options (`objectsPerFrame`, `editChecksPerFrame`). *Why:* a still scene must not cost CPU time in proportion to its size (target ≤ 0.2 ms). M3 (moving objects) revisits the latency.
  - **Textured materials in M1:** the adapter multiplies the GI material by each albedo and emissive texture's average colour (and folds in average metalness), because the core samples no textures until the M4 atlas.
  - **`GIVolume`** is the LightProbeGrid-shaped volume SPEC asks for (width, height, depth, nx, ny, nz; probes at cell centres).
- **`requiredLimits` also asks for 256-thread compute workgroups** (`maxComputeInvocationsPerWorkgroup` and `maxComputeWorkgroupSizeX`, capped at the adapter's value; M1 lead, from T1.6's compatibility-mode test). Compatibility-mode devices default to 128, and the platform's kernels use 256. If the device can't give it, `prepare` throws `GILimitError` naming the limit before compiling anything. Workgroup memory isn't raised: every kernel fits compatibility's default. Compatibility mode now has a GPU test page (`ddgi-shading-compat`), passing on both GPUs.
- **T1.6's "GI sampling ≤ 1.0 ms at 1440p" is judged on a view where every pixel is a surface, at normal GPU clocks** (M1 lead). Cornell's inside bookmark (2) is that worst case. The front view, with sky and background pixels, costs less and would flatter the number. A run with the RTX's clocks locked below normal doesn't count for or against the target.
- **DDGI coverage** (T1.6): 1 out to half a spacing beyond the outermost probes, fading to 0 over the next half spacing, multiplied by `saturate(Σw / 0.02)` so points no probe can see fade to the host's environment. *Why outside the probe box:* walls usually sit just outside the outermost probes (Cornell's are 3 cm out), and fading inside the box would dim them.
- **DDGI's big-change rule is off by default in M1; M2 drives fast response from scene changes instead** (M1 lead, after T1.5b).
  - Any rule that lowers hysteresis when a probe's new estimate looks very different reacts to ray noise too. Measured on Cornell (in-air volume, against the rule off): per texel +8%; on the probe's mean, applied to the same update, +22% (a light near the ceiling makes even the mean noisy and skewed); on the probe's mean, applied to the next update, −1.8 points, because it forgets bright spikes faster.
  - All three are biases, so the default is off (`irradianceChangeThreshold` disabled). The lagged probe-mean rule stays as an opt-in setting.
  - *Why not keep the one nearest the reference:* a bias that happens to cancel another on one scene doesn't generalise.
  - *M2:* light changes arrive through `SceneChanges`, so the technique lowers hysteresis for the probes near a known change, deterministically. That's how the ≤ 0.5 s light-response target gets met.
- **DDGI relocation and classification tuning** (T1.5b, each measured on both GPUs):
  - `minFrontFaceDistance` defaults to 0.02 of the smallest spacing (the design said 0.1): two-room leak 1.08% → 0.86%. Changing the view or normal bias didn't help; with no view bias the thin wall leaked 6.9%.
  - Relocation only moves a probe to a point inside the volume's probe box: two-room 0.86% → 0.78%.
  - "No surface nearby" uses a reach of 1.5 spacings, not 1: at 1.0, 25 Cornell probes that sampling needs were switched off. Costs Cornell about 1 point.
  - A probe also counts as inside geometry when the nearest thing its fixed rays see is a back face.
  - Known limit: where solids touch with shared faces (the cabinet row), 3–5 probes stay active while up to 3.4 mm inside a board. Ray-based inside tests can't see through coincident faces. The classification check reports these; anything deeper fails.

- **DDGI's update is split into a trace-and-shade kernel and a blend kernel, with ray results in a small buffer** (M1 lead, from T1.5a's measurement). The design (`docs/design/ddgi.md`) had one kernel that traced, shaded and blended with rays never leaving workgroup memory, and said to time it against a split version if the cost came near its budget. It did. At 512 probes × 256 rays: RTX 3060 2.48 ms one kernel against 1.22 ms split; Intel UHD 11.6 against 7.8 ms, with the same field. The single kernel is register-bound. Tracing stays inline: the trace kernel calls `giTraceClosest` itself. The platform's `encodeBatch` isn't used. T1.5b makes the switch.
- **T1.5a's Cornell accuracy is measured on a volume with every probe in the air. T1.5b must pass on the manifest's own volume** (M1 lead).
  - **Correction after the T1.5a review (2026-11-05):** probe placement is the smaller part of the excess. Most of it came from the design's change-threshold rule, tested per texel on noisy estimates. With the rule off: in-air +4.38% (RTX) / +4.31% (Intel), manifest volume +6.73%. The rule moves to the probe's mean irradiance (design doc). With that, Cornell is gated on the **manifest volume** again, and the in-air number becomes an extra report. The path-traced check's finding (true irradiance rises 12–21% over the first 0.5 m off the floor) still stands as the reason probe placement matters.
  - On the manifest volume one probe layer sits 1 cm inside the floor and another inside the ceiling. Without relocation (T1.5b) that reads +11.4% against the ±10% target. With probes in the air it reads +9.50% (RTX) and +9.43% (Intel).
  - A path-traced irradiance check traces the excess to probe placement, not to lost or created energy: true irradiance rises 12–21% over the first 0.5 m off the floor.
  - +9.5% is close to the limit. T1.5b's relocation and bias tuning are expected to bring it down. If they don't, it comes back as a decision.
- **DDGI rounds stochastically when it writes half-float atlases** (T1.5a). With hysteresis 0.97, round-to-nearest can't move a value by less than half a half-float step, so the furnace stalled 4.6% low. With stochastic rounding it settles 0.6% low.

- **The GPU top level: refit every frame, rebuild the shape in a worker** (B1a).
  - **Rebuild trigger:** an instance is created, or the tree's quality signal (relative SAH cost) passes 1.25× its as-built value. A planning number: the top level is a small share of trace work.
  - **The quality check runs in the worker, every 250 ms while anything moves** (B1a review). The platform promises no GPU readbacks by default, so the GPU refit's own quality numbers aren't read back. Instead the worker refits the current shape on the CPU from fresh transforms and rebuilds past 1.25×. Each check packs every instance's transform on the main thread: about 1.3 ms for 10k instances, once per 250 ms, as a single-frame spike. The rebuild itself (54–57 ms for 10k) is in the worker.
  - **A refit that gives up is repaired on the GPU in the same frame** by a one-workgroup pass, which costs about 2 µs when idle. When it has to run, the frame costs 8.4 ms (RTX) or 4.7 ms (Intel) at 10k instances, mostly the spin before giving up. That's accepted, because give-ups have never happened outside the forced test. *Revisit if* a device shows them in practice. Give-ups are counted and warned about only with `gpuTiming` on (no readbacks otherwise).
  - **New instances** become visible when their shape arrives (about 55 ms for 10k), as new meshes do. Hiding and showing take effect in the same frame.
  - **Without subgroups,** the tracer keeps the CPU top level, per the subgroups-first rule (2026-11-04). The refit itself uses subgroups only for its pairing step, worth 2% on the RTX and 8% on Intel. Dropping that step would give every device the GPU refit with one code path. Not done now, because every M1 device has subgroups. *Revisit* when a target device lacks them (Safari at the Apple tier).

- **A procedural sky gets a `sunIlluminance` field; `intensity` stays a plain multiplier everywhere.** Dennis: "i would look at what pmndrs/sky calls the prop but in general I think your lean sounds find".
  - Sky radiance = the calibrated sky × `sunIlluminance` × `intensity`. Both default to 1.
  - `sunIlluminance` is the same quantity as pmndrs/sky's `exposure` (`luminanceScale`, default 40 there), which computes its sky for a sun of illuminance 1 and then scales it. We don't call it `exposure`, because "exposure" already means the camera EV (the per-scene default in T1.8). `turbidity` already matches pmndrs/sky's name.
  - The three adapter (T1.7) maps a pmndrs/sky instance's `exposure` to `sunIlluminance`, or otherwise takes it from the scene's sun `DirectionalLight` intensity.
  - *Why:* one field, one meaning. T1.4 had briefly made a procedural sky's `intensity` mean the sun's illuminance, while every other `intensity` is a multiplier.
- **The platform's sky uses SH bands 0, 1, 2 and 4 (18 coefficients) for irradiance, and a 256×128 equirect radiance map for rays that miss** (T1.4).
  - *Why bands 0–2 + 4:* L2 SH (bands 0–2) missed the ±0.5% sky tolerance on our skies (+0.74 / −0.33 / −0.87% on open ground). Band 4 brings it to +0.30 / −0.11 / −0.25%. Band 3 adds nothing to irradiance, because its cosine-convolution weight is 0.
  - *Why an equirect map,* not the "small octahedral map" SPEC and the old contract described: it matches the sky files' layout and three's `equirectUV`, so there's one convention end to end. At 256×128 it's about 0.5 MB.
  - *Revisit if:* memory gets tight on Tier B, or the sun's glow needs more resolution in reflections.

- **Dennis on three open questions:** "go with a for exposure, candles as separate scene. regareding the limit, threejs and other apps routinely go over that. feel free."
  - **Exposure:** scenes get a default exposure in their manifest (Sponza about +2 EV), and the sky stays physically calibrated. The exact values are picked in T1.8, once our DDGI is in the viewer, since the probe-grid baseline may mislead.
  - **Candles:** a separate opt-in `sponza-candles` scene with a stated subset of the 10,000 lights, for M2's many-lights stress test. `sponza-full` stays base + curtains + ivy + trees.
  - **Device limits:** raising limits above the defaults is fine, and `sponza` stays base + curtains.

    - A scene that doesn't fit throws `GILimitError` naming the limit.
    - Which scenes need raised limits, and by how much, is recorded in NOTES.

## 2026-11-04

- **Sky brightness: the Ineichen–Perez clear-sky ratio** (T1.1). A flat open ground gets sky irradiance equal to the Ineichen–Perez 2002 clear-sky diffuse-to-direct horizontal ratio times the sun's horizontal illuminance (sea level, Kasten–Young air mass, Linke turbidity taken from the manifest's `turbidity`, no Perez enhancement, as in pvlib's default). Sponza's sky gives the ground 12.3% of the sun's light on it.
  - *Why:* it's a measured-data model, and it changes with sun elevation and haze.
  - *Revisit if:* Dennis wants brighter interiors. Adjust `environment.intensity` or the exposure, not the ratio.
- **T1.1's viewer check means "the viewer shows the same sky file, the right way round". Sky-lighting accuracy is judged on the platform's own sky irradiance.** (M1 lead, after the T1.1 review)
  - The viewer never lights surfaces with three's environment (PMREM); only the GI technique does. So the open-ground PMREM comparison (−0.65% brightness against ±0.5%) measured a mode the viewer doesn't use. PMREM is a GGX lobe, not a cosine integral (NOTES).
  - The accuracy check moves to where it matters: T1.4 holds the platform's `giSkyIrradiance` to the reference tolerance (±0.5%) against Cycles on open ground, and T1.8 repeats it through DDGI in the viewer. The tolerance itself is unchanged.
- **The native harness gets the sky as one flat colour in M1** (the file's flat equivalent, E/π). Its shaders have one constant `skyRadiance` and no texture path. M1 compares native on timings, not images, so a texture sky in the fork's miss shader moves to M2 (sky and time of day).
- **The scene copy stores normals octahedral-encoded (4 bytes) and indices as 16 bits wherever every index fits** (T1.2). This settles open gap 9 in `docs/api/platform.md`.
  - *Why:* measured vertex buffers: Sponza 87.7 MB against 104.1 MB with float normals; Bistro exterior 111.4 MB against 134.8 MB, so it fits the 128 MB default storage binding only this way.
  - *Revisit if:* hit shading needs normals more precise than about 1e-4 rad.
- **Front faces follow the side the mesh was authored with, also for mirrored instances** (M1 lead, from the T1.2 review). three.js keeps a mirrored mesh's authored front side. The tracer's `GiHit.frontFace` and the scene copy's geometric normal followed world-space winding, which a mirror flips. So a ray hitting a mirrored closed mesh from outside would count as a back face, and DDGI's "more than 25% back faces" rule would switch off probes next to it. T1.3 changes the tracer and `giInstanceGeometricNormal` together, and the oracle's mirrored cases test it.
