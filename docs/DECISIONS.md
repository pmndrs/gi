- **Known miss (T3.8, one attempt): a camera-following volume's teleport after start still runs a full-rate warm-up for seconds** (T3.8 implementer, for the lead; Apple M5 Pro, metal-3, Chrome headless, `scenario-shots.ts --only fly-through --sample`, untimed). *The attempt:* the jump right after DDGI starts is gone. `GIVolume.start`/`restart` take the host's `camera`, and the viewer passes its own, so the window warms up where the camera is: in the 6 s after the link opens Bistro's fly-through, 174-236 probes a frame (2.7-4.1 ms DDGI GPU) against 4,666-8,192 (41-55 ms) before. *What is left:* any later jump of more than half the window (choosing the fly-through again mid-flight, a far bookmark with the camera volume on) still resets every slot and warms up every volume at up to 8,192 probes a frame: 3-4 s at 27-39 ms after the fix, 5-6 s at 31-60 ms before (M5 Pro, two runs each); about 9 s at 65 ms on the RTX 3060 Laptop (T3.8's first run). The flight itself is unaffected (round trip, no loop jump). *The fix, not tried here:* a teleport warms only its own volume, for the 2 updates an entering probe owes (`ENTERING_WARMING_UPDATES`), about 3 frames for 12,288 probes, instead of `startWarmUp()` over every volume (ddgi-technique.ts `followCamera`). *Revisit when* the tuning pass reaches the camera volume, or Dennis notices the stall in the viewer.
- **Saved bakes: the `.ddgibake` format, version 1, and its rules** (T5.2 implementer; docs/design/m5-coverage.md §2; for the lead's review). The file is `DDGB`, a `u32` version, a `u32` header length, a JSON header, then the sections on 256-byte boundaries (techniques/ddgi/saved-field.ts; docs/api/platform.md "Saved bakes"). The calls the design left open:
  - *A camera-following volume's window is saved, not refused:* its lattice position and its valid extent go in the header and are put back at load (`CameraWindow.restore`). Slots follow from the lattice, so the saved slots line up. A camera far from the saved window is a teleport and starts that volume over, as any teleport does.
  - *The probe history is settled when saved:* it keeps each probe's sample count, update count, last-update time, mean and noise, and drops owed updates, marks, wakes, frame stamps and the change clock. The technique's clock is saved with it (`clockSeconds`) and set back at load, so the histories age from where they were.
  - *The first frame after a load doesn't mark the scene changes it brings:* the fingerprint was checked against the scene with them, and on a fresh platform they are the scene's creation (every object and light added), which cut the loaded histories (Cornell's view moved 2.2% in that frame, against 0.002% for the saved page's own next frame). A change made between `loadField` and the next frame goes unmarked.
  - *A stale load marks every probe as a whole change, r = 1* (`STALE_FIELD_CHANGE`, as `OVERFLOW_CHANGE`), in the first frame that updates probes. Each probe is owed the updates a whole change needs (14 on two-room) and takes the new light in from the loaded value. *Why not earlier:* marked in the tree-wait frame, which updates nothing, `changeServing` judged two-room by a batch of 0 as a volume too big for every probe's updates and sent the change down the key-probe path; the view then stalled 18-23% from settled and took over 240 frames, against 21 for the same lamp change made live. *Why not `MAX_GLOBAL_CHANGE`:* it owes 34 updates a probe (`ddgiNeededUpdates`) for the same result.
  - *Distance formats are converted, not refused:* `rg16float` and `rgba16float` hold the same two halves, so a bake moves between devices with and without `texture-formats-tier1` exactly. The format is recorded but isn't part of the match.
  - *No rotation is recorded:* volumes are axis-aligned boxes; a rotation would be a format change.
  - *The fingerprint* hashes geometry, materials, textures, lights (with `castsShadows`) and the sky per part (platform/scene/scene-fingerprint.ts). Textures over 1 MB are hashed from an even 1 MB sample (hashing runs on the host thread when a texture or mesh is created, 1.9 ns a word on the M5 Pro); `ImageBitmap`, canvases and `GPUTexture`s by size, format and colour space only (no cheap CPU read), image elements by URL and size; a texture environment by its texture's shape. A deforming mesh counts with its rest pose. Labels never count.
  - *The `ddgi-saved-bake` page's bar for "settles back within the usual response":* the stale load is within 5% of the changed scene's settled mean, and stays, within 1.5 × the frames the same change takes made live, plus 10 frames.
  - *Revisit when:* a host needs bakes across devices with other tile sizes or layer counts (a resample at load), a repainted `ImageBitmap` has to be noticed (hash the texture store's layer on the GPU), or a host loads a bake while changing the scene in the same frame.

The per-fragment path (`GIVolume`, used by the viewer) lights the surfaces behind before three's transmission samples them, so it is fine. *Why not fixed now:* the composite would need the GI of the surface behind each glass pixel, a new input from three's transmission pass; that is tuning-pass work. *Revisit:* in the tuning pass, or when a host asks for the composite with glass.

- **`ddgi-textured-furnace`'s textures-off limit is 0.5%, not 0.1%** (lead, 2026-11-10, after the first Mac run; this settles T4.5's open "Intel furnace limit" question). The 0.1% was set on the RTX alone (+0.006%). The M5 Pro reads −0.26% and the Intel UHD −0.37%. The furnace's own error differs by about 0.3% from GPU to GPU (flat albedo: RTX −0.15%, M5 Pro −0.22%, Intel −0.28%), so no limit under that spread can hold on every device. Subtracting the flat furnace's error doesn't help: the RTX would then read +0.16%. *Why 0.5% still checks something:* a textures-off path that read the wrong mip, or skipped the texture, moves the mean far more than that. The plain furnace's 2% bar is unchanged. *Revisit if:* a device reads over 0.5% while its flat furnace is within 0.3%.

- **A camera volume's entering probes owe 2 updates, not the design's K** (lead, from T3.6's review; m3-moving.md §4.2 asked for K). Two blended updates is the same warm-up rule moved and woken probes already use (m3-moving.md 5.2, T2.M1). K entering planes' worth of probes every few frames would starve the rest of the window at a 5 m/s fly-through, and the 10-spacing fade (above) already hides the first updates' noise. *Revisit if:* entering probes show visible noise at the fade's far edge.

- **Deforming meshes' readbacks: the refit's stats at most once every 15 frames per posed mesh, and a drift rebuild's own build; confirmed by Dennis on 2026-11-18 ("Confirm both", the 2026-11-18 entry above)** (lead ruling from T3.3's review; widens the T3.2 entry below, 2026-11-09; was provisional until then). While a deforming mesh is being posed, its refit's stats (a few words: the relative SAH cost the held-drift rule reads) are copied out at most once every 15 frames and read back a few frames later; a pose held still makes none. A drift rebuild (at most one a frame, rare by design) submits its own H-PLOC build and reads back that one tree's stats and layout (the refit needs its shape). SPEC's exception and platform.md say so. *Why:* the held-drift rule (2026-11-09) needs the refit's cost, which only the GPU has for GPU poses; one sample every 15 frames still gives four or five inside the 60 frames a drift must hold, where copying after every refit read back every 2-3 frames. *Revisit if* a host can't allow it: the CPU form could compute the cost itself (`refitOnCpu`), and the GPU form would then rebuild on a timer or not at all.

- **A camera-following volume fades over 10 spacings, not half a spacing, so its moving edge meets the stability threshold** (coordinator's ruling on T3.6's first result: one attempt at a slower fade, 2026-11-10). The band sweeps over the scene at the camera's speed, so how long a point takes to fade is the band's width over that speed: half a spacing was 0.15 s at 5 m/s and 1.5 m, and on Bistro's fly-through (`ddgi-camera-volume`, RTX 3060 Laptop; 32 × 12 × 32 at 1.5 m in front of the manifest's 4 m volume; 66 m at 5 m/s, 264 probes a frame) the band changed **0.59% a frame (mean), p95 2.6%** against the bar's 0.1% / 0.5%: the camera volume's share stepped 0.084 a frame across the 1.5 m and 4 m fields' ~18% difference. `CAMERA_FADE_SPACINGS` = 10 (scroll.ts) takes 3 s at that speed: the same run reads **0.074% / 0.27%** (the part that moved that frame 0.107% / 0.38%; share step 0.0045 a frame; at rest 0.008% / 0.029%), and the other five checks still pass. A short axis gets a narrower fade, keeping a spacing of full coverage around the camera (the 12-probe vertical axis: 3.75). Fixed volumes keep half a spacing and read as before. *What it costs:* the camera volume answers alone only within about 5 spacings of the camera on a long axis (7 m at 1.5 m), and blends with what lies behind it out to the window's ends. *Still seen:* the largest step of the share at one band pixel in one frame was 0.83 (at rest 0.22), so a few pixels still jump: likely a probe that finishes warming late inside the fade (a plane counts as warm with up to 1/32 cold), not the edge. *Revisit if:* T3.8's viewer fly-through shows the wide blend or those jumps to the eye, or a scene's camera volume needs its full detail farther out (a larger window, or the fade from the camera's speed).

## 2026-11-09

- **T3.4: the far-set guess is sized by the box's projected area and weighed by contrast; Ω_ref is a hemisphere** (T3.4 implementer; for the lead's review; replaces T2.M1's Ω / 0.5 sr with the bounding sphere). A far probe's change is `r = min(1, Ω / 2π × contrast) × min(1, moved / spacing)`. Ω is the area of the box's faces facing the probe over the distance to its centre squared. Contrast is the brightest stored mean among the active probes within 1.5 spacings of the box's centre over the probe's own, at least 1.
  - *Why not the size alone, as the 2026-11-06 entry planned:* scored offline on T2.M1's marks dumped per probe (NOTES), no rule on solid angle or reach separates the two cases. The door's far probes that really changed see it at smaller angles than the cabinet's wrong marks. What differs is contrast: the dark room's light comes through the doorway. The stored distances can't tell an opening from a wall (they stop at the distance clamp).
  - *Offline, same dumps:* the cabinet's far set 826 → 442 at the same far recall (106 of 112); the door swing's far recall 90.0% → 89.3%; the one-step door close's far recall 90.0% → 68.0% (its near set is unchanged).
  - *On the RTX 3060 (T3.4's code):* the cabinet's 1 m slide marks 645 probes (T2.M1: 993; far set 478), recall 97.4% (T2.M1: 98.1%), precision 40% (26%), and its changed pixels are within 5% of settled from frame 15 (deadline 60). The door swing's recall is 91.5%, precision 97.7% (T2.M1: 91.5% / 97.7%); its leak 0.157% at 1 s. The one-step close's recall is 72.3% (91.4%), precision 99.3%, and it leaks the same as before: 2.44% in the frame it shuts, within 1% from frame 4, 0.325% at 1 s (36668bc, same run conditions: 2.47%, frame 4, 0.315%).
  - *Revisit if:* a moved object in front of a bright window or a lamp, seen from a lit room, shows a slow response (the contrast reads the light around the box's centre, not what the probe sees through it).

- **T3.4: woken air probes, as m3-moving.md §5.3 has them: 2 spacings, 300 frames; receivers, moves and poses wake, objects created, destroyed, shown or hidden don't** (T3.4 implementer). *Why not whole changes:* a scene's first frame creates every object, and a room mesh's box holds every probe, so every air probe would be awake for the first 5 s, through the warm-up and Budget v2's calibration, which would then calibrate a different scene from M2's. A new GI object's own surfaces switch the probes near it on by classification anyway. *Known limit:* a receiver that stops in mid-air isn't reported again (the change feed lists receivers that moved), so its probes go back to sleep 5 s later and it loses its GI. *Revisit* when a host needs still receivers in mid-air lit: the platform would have to list receivers that stand still, or the wake would have to last while a receiver's box holds it.

- **A camera-following volume's floor adds the warming its entering planes owe, served from the whole frame's probes; accepted by the coordinator 2026-11-10, with the 1/32 warm-plane rule below** (T3.6 implementer; the revisit named in "a scheduler per volume" below). Beside Bistro's 29k-probe 4 m volume, the camera volume's share by probe count was about 80 of 264 probes a frame, while a diagonal flight at 5 m/s enters about 4.7 planes a second (about 60 warming updates a frame); its valid extent then stalled and, 33 m into the flight, the camera volume covered nothing. Now its floor adds `warmingDemand`, 2 updates per entering probe averaged over 30 frames (scroll.ts), and with one volume the hand-out is raised to that floor, as Budget v2's floor is. Also: a plane counts as warm with at most 1/32 of its probes still in state 3 (one late probe held the extent back for good). *Revisit if:* the demand makes a still-camera frame cost more (it decays to 0 within about a second of stopping), or a global probe numbering arrives.

- **`trace-runtime-trees` allows 3% ambiguous oracle answers instead of the oracle's usual 1%; mismatches are never excused** (lead, from T3.2's review). Every ray on that page is aimed at 18 mm boards, so many land on edges shared by two faces, where either face is a correct answer; the first run read 1.25%. The allowance is on that page only: `oracle-compare.ts`, `trace-oracle` and `tlas-oracle` stay at 1%. *Revisit if:* a page change makes its rays less edge-heavy.

- **Meshes added or replaced while the scene runs get GPU trees too, and a frame that uploads meshes submits that build itself and reads back its stats; confirmed by Dennis on 2026-11-18 ("Confirm both", the 2026-11-18 entry)** (T3.2 implementer; replaces 2026-11-08's "GPU builds are only for the scene as first loaded", which left runtime builds to T3.2). Every frame's uploaded meshes (`createMesh`, `updateMesh`, and for now each pose of a deforming mesh, until T3.3's refit) go into one H-PLOC batch, submitted in a command buffer of the platform's own with a stats readback of a few words per mesh, exactly as at load (the 2026-11-08 entries). This widens SPEC's exception from "once, at load" to "frames that upload meshes"; frames that upload nothing still submit nothing and read nothing back. *Why:* m3-moving.md §2.2 plans it, and recording the build into the host's encoder instead would need the readback started a frame later, after the host's submit: one to two more frames before rays see the mesh, against a 4-frame bar. Also: **one tinybvh build per mesh at a time**; a mesh replaced again while its build runs keeps only its newest version waiting. *Why:* the worker builds in order, and once GPU trees let a host reshape a mesh every few frames, a build per upload would queue faster than the worker clears it. *Revisit if* a host can't allow the submits, or T3.3's refit makes runtime rebuilds rare enough to record them in the host's encoder.

- **Several DDGI volumes: a scheduler per volume, not one global probe numbering, for M3** (lead ruling on T3.5's review, 2026-11-09; departs from m3-moving.md §4.1, which planned one global index across volumes so a single scheduler, history and update list served all). As built, each volume runs DDGI's kernels with its own uniforms, update list, history and scheduler, one volume after another in a frame. Each frame a volume gets Budget v2's floor of its own (a full sweep of it within 4 s, never 0 probes a frame), and the rest of the hand-out is shared by probe count, with every volume's fraction carried over frames so a small volume beside a big one is never starved (volumes.ts, `UnitSharer`, `volumeFloorUnits`). *Why:* the kernels then see one volume at a time and change only where they touch the atlases (a layer base), which kept T3.5 contained in `ddgi-technique.ts` for T3.4's rebase and needed no global index in mark, schedule, propagate or blend. *What it costs:* priorities don't compete across volumes: a change near the camera in one volume can't take probes from another volume's quiet ones, and each volume pays its own scheduler passes. *Revisit if:* a scene shows one volume's urgent probes waiting while another spends its share on quiet ones (a response check in T3.8 or T3.9 misses because of it), or the camera-following volume (T3.6) needs its entering planes served from the whole frame's probes.

- **A deforming mesh's tree is rebuilt only when its refit has drifted for about a second, not at the first sign of drift** (lead, from T3.0's spike; this changes m3-moving.md §3.1's 1.1× rule). Refit quality follows how far the pose is from rest, not how long refitting has gone on. After 600 frames the tree is byte for byte one refit of that pose. In a breeze, SAH is 1.00–1.07× and trace speed is within noise. In a gust, SAH is 1.03–1.43× and closest hits are 1.15–1.89× slower. A plain 1.1× trigger would fire all through a gust, rebuilding a tree that the next frame's pose makes stale again. The bound: a colour or intensity change alone is |new/old − 1| (old/new − 1 for a dimming, as T2.4b measures); a point light moved by d adds the magnitude change (R/(R+d))² to ((R+d)/R)² and the turn asin(d/R); anything that can switch light on or off far away (on or off, a spot turned or moved, a range set, a move of R or more) is a whole change (`MAX_GLOBAL_CHANGE`). - **A change's cut holds for the updates it promised and is solved with a bounce share, `changeBounceShare` 0.8** (T2.4b implementer; for the M2 lead's review). This replaces the design's `n ≤ εK / (r − ε)` with the count growing back by one an update (m2-lights.md §4.1).
  - *Why:* each new estimate bounces off a field that still holds the old light, so the error shrinks by `h + (1 − h)ρ` an update, not by `h`; with the count growing back the probe averages those stale estimates, and the error shrinks only like k^−(1−ρ). Two-room's lamp halved at its calibrated 880 probes a frame was still 45% of the step away after 30 frames. This was also T2.4a's lingering overshoot.
  - *The rule:* `h = (c − ρ)/(1 − ρ)`, `c = (ε/r)^(1/K)`, held for the promised updates; a change promises `max(K, ⌈ln(ε/e)/ln ρ⌉)` updates (e: its error against the new light), because even h = 0 shrinks the error by only ρ an update.
  - *0.8:* two-room's ρ, measured from that halving (the error shrank 0.84 a frame with 82% of the probes updated at h ≈ 0). Cornell fits 0.65 (T1.12); open scenes are lower and get somewhat deeper cuts than they need (more noise for a few updates).

- **Cornell's flicker: the fix in M1 is per-probe adaptive hysteresis; sampling emitters directly goes to M2** (M1 lead, after T1.12 phase 1).
  - *Cause (measured):* ray noise, not a bug. Probes find the emissive Cornell's small, bright ceiling emitter only by brute-force rays (about 3 of 224 per probe), so one update's texel estimate is off by σ = 30% of the atlas mean. Hysteresis 0.97 leaves a slow wobble of about 3.6% per texel (theory σ√((1−h)/(1+h)) = 3.8%; per-frame change predicted 0.94%, measured 0.91%). Probe states, offsets, rounding, the firefly clamp and bounce light add nothing. Fewer probes per frame only slow the wobble.
  - *M1 fix:* each probe's hysteresis rises with its own history. A new probe averages its updates evenly (h = 1 − 1/n) until it reaches a high cap (about 0.995) on a still scene, so it converges fast at first and then settles. Per-probe noise can raise or lower the cap. Light and geometry changes still need M2's change-driven reset (SceneChanges). Dennis judges the result by eye.
  - *M2:* sample emissive surfaces directly in the probe update (next-event estimation over an emissive-triangle list), which removes the noise at its source. It goes with the area lights already carried to M2. *Not built (noted 2026-11-18):* emitters still light the scene only through rays that hit them; area lights are in TASKS Later, and platform.md says so.
  - *Not chosen:* only raising h to 0.99 (3× quieter but 3× slower to converge); 512 rays (about 2× the update cost for 0.54× the flicker); a screen-space denoiser (pmndrs/denoiser `feat/rt-denoiser-cleanroom` SVGF lists 4.0 ms at 1080p, over the 1.0 ms bar, and its 13–32-frame history is shorter than the wobble, so it would hide the noise rather than remove it).
- **Hysteresis knows the time between a probe's updates (T1.12 phase 2a, M1 lead, 2026-11-05).** Each probe keeps an effective sample count n and its last update time; on an update n fades by `exp(−Δt / τ)`, the blend uses h = n / (n + 1), then n ← min(n + 1, 200). **On by default, τ = 5 s, n_max = 200 (h ≤ 0.995).** The technique's clock sums `deltaTime`, capped at 0.25 s a frame so a hidden tab doesn't wipe the history.
  - *Measured (RTX):* Cornell flicker (mean per-pixel change) 0.56% → 0.095%; Sponza flicker 0.0038% → 0.027% (sparse probes keep fewer samples, still far below Cornell's); Sponza after a restart to within 1%: 44 s → 3 s; Sponza after halving the sun: to within 1% 162.5 s → 22.5 s.
  - *Why 5 s:* Cornell reaches the cap at any τ above about 3.4 s; 5 s follows Sponza's light change twice as fast as 10 s and is half as noisy as 2 s.
  - *Cost:* probes updated every frame settle bounce light more slowly (Cornell 17.5 s to within 1% after warm-up).
  - *Tests that changed with it:* the furnace page runs 1,800 frames (was 600; before the warm-up ramp the Intel read −2.05% against the 2% limit at 600 while still converging; with the ramp it settles by 600, so 1,800 is a margin, not a need) and the two-room leak page 3,000 (timeout 600 s; a longer run reads a slightly higher leak, so it is the conservative choice).
  - **The clock wraps every 4,096 s** (about 68 min), so per-probe times stay below 2^12 s where f32 steps 0.49 ms; the blend adds 4,096 s back when `T − t` comes out negative. It would misread only a probe left unblended for more than 68 min while active (active probes update every sweep, seconds apart).
  - **The warm-up ramp:** for a probe's first K = 16 blended updates its count is capped at k / a with a = 8 (h = k / (k + 8)), then it grows by one per update as before. *Why:* a plain running average whose bounce light reads the field it is still building left Cornell 26% low after the 16-pass warm-up and took 19 s to get within 1%; the ramp weights the later, brighter estimates more: Cornell −7% after warm-up, within 1% in 1 s; settled flicker unchanged (Cornell 0.094%, Sponza 0.025%); Sponza after halving the sun within 1% in 20.5 s. K matches the warm-up's 16 passes; an endless a = 8 kept Cornell noisy for about 27 s.

  - **Textured materials in M1:** the adapter multiplies the GI material by each albedo and emissive texture's average colour (and folds in average metalness), because the core samples no textures until the M4 atlas.
  - **`GIVolume`** is the LightProbeGrid-shaped volume SPEC asks for (width, height, depth, nx, ny, nz; probes at cell centres).
- **`requiredLimits` also asks for 256-thread compute workgroups** (`maxComputeInvocationsPerWorkgroup` and `maxComputeWorkgroupSizeX`, capped at the adapter's value; M1 lead, from T1.6's compatibility-mode test). Compatibility-mode devices default to 128, and the platform's kernels use 256. If the device can't give it, `prepare` throws `GILimitError` naming the limit before compiling anything. - **T1.6's "GI sampling ≤ 1.0 ms at 1440p" is judged on a view where every pixel is a surface, at normal GPU clocks** (M1 lead). Cornell's inside bookmark (2) is that worst case. The front view, with sky and background pixels, costs less and would flatter the number. A run with the RTX's clocks locked below normal doesn't count for or against the target.
- **DDGI coverage** (T1.6): 1 out to half a spacing beyond the outermost probes, fading to 0 over the next half spacing, multiplied by `saturate(Σw / 0.02)` so points no probe can see fade to the host's environment. *Why outside the probe box:* walls usually sit just outside the outermost probes (Cornell's are 3 cm out), and fading inside the box would dim them.
- **DDGI's big-change rule is off by default in M1; M2 drives fast response from scene changes instead** (M1 lead, after T1.5b).
  - Any rule that lowers hysteresis when a probe's new estimate looks very different reacts to ray noise too. Measured on Cornell (in-air volume, against the rule off): per texel +8%; on the probe's mean, applied to the same update, +22% (a light near the ceiling makes even the mean noisy and skewed); on the probe's mean, applied to the next update, −1.8 points, because it forgets bright spikes faster.
  - All three are biases, so the default is off (`irradianceChangeThreshold` disabled). The lagged probe-mean rule stays as an opt-in setting.
  - *Why not keep the one nearest the reference:* a bias that happens to cancel another on one scene doesn't generalise.
  - *M2:* light changes arrive through `SceneChanges`, so the technique lowers hysteresis for the probes near a known change, deterministically. That's how the ≤ 0.5 s light-response target gets met.
- **DDGI relocation and classification tuning** (T1.5b, each measured on both GPUs):
  - `minFrontFaceDistance` defaults to 0.02 of the smallest spacing (the design said 0.1): two-room leak 1.08% → 0.86%. Changing the view or normal bias didn't help; with no view bias the thin wall leaked 6.9%.
  - Relocation only moves a probe to a point inside the volume's probe box: two-room 0.86% → 0.78%.
  - "No surface nearby" uses a reach of 1.5 spacings, not 1: at 1.0, 25 Cornell probes that sampling needs were switched off. Costs Cornell about 1 point.

  - Known limit: where solids touch with shared faces (the cabinet row), 3–5 probes stay active while up to 3.4 mm inside a board. Ray-based inside tests can't see through coincident faces. The classification check reports these; anything deeper fails.

- **The platform's sky uses SH bands 0, 1, 2 and 4 (18 coefficients) for irradiance, and a 256×128 equirect radiance map for rays that miss** (T1.4).

- **Two ground-truth renderers must agree before we trust either.** Cycles (CPU) and three-gpu-pathtracer count as agreeing on a scene when, compared with `tools/compare` after 8×8-pixel block averaging:
  - **full image:** overall brightness within ±0.5%, block error at most 1%;
  - **indirect only:** brightness within ±1%, block error at most 3%.
  - Cornell measured -0.002% / 0.37% (full) and -0.014% / 1.43% (indirect). Two-room measured +0.11% and +0.12% brightness.
  - *Why these numbers:*
    - Block averaging cancels per-pixel noise. Two Cycles seeds already differ by 0.8% per pixel, so per-pixel limits would only measure noise.
    - The limits are about 2–3× what we measured. That leaves room for noise, while a wrong light unit, a missing bounce or a flipped axis would still fail: those show up as 5% or more, as the 12% light-range gap on two-room did.
    - DDGI's own targets against these references will be several percent, so references agreeing within 0.5–1% is accurate enough to judge DDGI.
  - *Revisit if:* a run sits near a limit. In that case, measure the path tracer's own seed-to-seed noise; it wasn't measured in T0.7, and its noise likely makes up most of the 0.37%. Also revisit if DDGI targets tighter than about 3% need sharper references.
